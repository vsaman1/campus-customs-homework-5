Reflection

Run outcome

1. Agent Performance

Ticket 101

The agents performed well. Boss correctly brought in Inventory because the central question was whether the size-S Bulldog tee could actually be fulfilled. Inventory confirmed that zero units were in stock and identified the $840 Bulldog Print Co invoice with a five-day lead time. The team stopped before making the payment and waited for my approval. After I approved the payment and separately approved resolution, the ticket was resolved. I especially appreciated that the agents did not promise inventory that was not available.

Ticket 102

Boss brought in Accounting, which confirmed the $2,400 rent amount, the due date, and that checking had enough cash to pay it. The system correctly required my approval before the payment and a separate approval before resolution. Checking fell from $2,560 to $160, which matched the database. This was accurate and appropriately cautious.

Ticket 103

The agents also handled this well after the workflow fixes. Inventory and Accounting confirmed that Yale AI Club wanted 20 medium hoodies, but only 8 were available, with a $22 unit cost and a $58 list price. Instead of inventing a discount or promising the other 12 units, the system stopped and asked me to decide. I approved no discount, retained the $58 price, and authorized an offer for only the 8 available units. This was a strong example of agents gathering facts while leaving the business decision to a human.

2. Actual Versus Expected Plan

Ticket 101

The actual result was close to the expected plan. I expected Inventory to be the first specialist because this was primarily a stock problem, and that happened. I also expected the overdue Bulldog Print Co invoice to matter, and it did. The main difference was that Accounting did not need to run as a separate agent in the clean run because Boss could use MCP evidence and the human approval flow directly.

Ticket 102

The actual result differed slightly from the expected plan. I originally thought Facilities would be called first because this was a rent and lease issue, but the clean run used Boss and Accounting instead. In hindsight, that was more efficient because MCP could retrieve the lease facts directly and the key decision was whether the $2,400 payment could be made safely.

Ticket 103

The actual result was very close to my plan. Inventory and Accounting were both useful: Inventory explained the 12-unit shortage, while Accounting explained the $22 cost and $58 list price. The biggest difference was that I originally expected another vendor or payment issue, but the final resolution required no additional payment. Instead, the agents stopped and asked me to make the pricing and fulfillment decision.

3. Work That Could Be Simpler With One Agent

Ticket 102 could probably have been handled by one agent with tools. Its core workflow was simple: check the lease, due date, and cash balance; prepare the payment; and wait for human approval. Multiple agents were not necessary to reason about separate parts of that problem.

Ticket 101 also could have been mostly handled by one operations agent because the inventory and vendor-invoice facts were tightly connected. The multi-agent structure was more useful for Ticket 103, where the inventory shortage and financial discount decision were genuinely different questions.

4. New Problems the Current Team Can Solve

Another stockout with an unpaid vendor invoice

For example, if a customer wanted a size-L sweatshirt that was out of stock and the supplier had an unpaid invoice, Inventory could check stock and vendor information while Accounting could evaluate the invoice and cash. The current tools already support this kind of workflow.

Another upcoming rent or lease payment

If Campus Customs received another rent notice, the team could check the lease, current date, cash balance, and existing payments, then prepare the payment for human approval. This would be very similar to Ticket 102.

Another bulk-order discount request

If a student organization wanted 30 shirts at a discount, the agents could check available inventory, unit cost, list price, and requested quantity. They could then present the pricing and stock facts to a human for the final discount and fulfillment decision, similar to Ticket 103.

5. New Problems the Current Team Cannot Solve

Shipment tracking

If a customer asked where a shipped order was, the current system would not have shipment or carrier-tracking data. I would add a Logistics Agent and tools for shipment status, tracking numbers, carrier scans, and estimated delivery dates.

Refunds and returns

The current tools do not include full order history or refund-processing capabilities. I would add tools for completed customer orders, return-eligibility validation, and refund processing. Customer Service could handle the customer interaction while Accounting handled the financial side.

Payroll or scheduling issues

The current database and agents do not include employee schedules, wages, hours, or payroll information. I would add an HR or People Agent with tools for employee records, schedules, time worked, and payroll, keeping employee information separate from shop-operation tools.
